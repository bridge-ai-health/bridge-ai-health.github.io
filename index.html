Bridge Program Overview

Knowledge Graph-guided Agentic AI: Bridging Reasoning and Explainability in Healthcare is an AAAI-27 Bridge Program exploring knowledge graphs as a shared interface between agentic reasoning and explainability, with healthcare as a central application. We bring together researchers and practitioners in AI, knowledge representation, causal reasoning, human–computer interaction, and healthcare.

Agentic systems plan, retrieve information, use tools, coordinate with other agents, and adapt. Understanding their decisions means connecting those actions to the evidence behind them. This bridge asks how we can make agent trajectories traceable, testable, and understandable to the people who rely on them. Graphs alone do not guarantee faithful explanations: their claims must be grounded in recorded events and tested against evidence.

The bridge combines tutorials, hands-on labs, research presentations, and interdisciplinary discussions to develop a shared graph vocabulary, evaluation and benchmark designs, and a community research roadmap. It is planned as a one-day, in-person event in Montréal during the February 16–17, 2027 Bridge Program window. The exact day will be announced.

Topics of Interest

We invite technical, clinical, interdisciplinary, and early-stage contributions. Relevant topics include, but are not limited to, the following.

  • Knowledge Graphs and Agentic Reasoning. Healthcare knowledge graphs, clinical ontologies, and graph-based memory; Knowledge graph-guided planning, retrieval, and decision support; Multimodal agents integrating clinical text, electronic health records, medical images, signals, radiation dose, and patient-reported outcomes; Multi-agent collaboration, delegation, communication, and responsibility attribution; Retrieval-augmented generation and graph-based retrieval for healthcare agents; Neuro-symbolic reasoning and integration of structured and unstructured medical knowledge.
  • Explainability, Causality, and Trustworthiness. Evidence-grounded explanations of agent decisions and trajectories; Provenance, traceability, uncertainty, and confidence calibration; Causal, contrastive, counterfactual, and why/why-not explanations; Evaluation of reasoning faithfulness, completeness, stability, and clinical usefulness; Human-in-the-loop oversight, intervention, and interactive explanation; Privacy, fairness, robustness, safety, and assurance of healthcare agents; Detection and mitigation of hallucinations, unsupported claims, and automation bias.
  • Clinical Translation and Applications. Clinical decision support, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment planning, and monitoring; Radiotherapy, precision oncology, critical care, medical imaging, and public health; Patient-reported outcomes and personalized supportive care; Clinical workflow integration and user-friendly agent interfaces; Barriers to adoption, including data quality, interoperability, regulation, usability, and trust; Clinician-, patient-, developer-, and auditor-specific explanation requirements; Prospective validation, deployment, and evaluation in real-world healthcare settings.

Call for Papers & Participation

This one-day, in-person bridge will combine introductory tutorials, invited presentations, selected position talks, posters, research papers, an Oxford-style debate, and small-group schema and benchmark-design sprints. Participants will work in interdisciplinary teams to develop shared terminology, graph representations, evaluation protocols, reporting criteria, and a roadmap for trustworthy healthcare agents.

We invite position papers, extended abstracts, challenge statements, demonstrations, dataset or benchmark descriptions, research papers, and early-stage research contributions. Submissions should describe the problem, its relevance to the bridge, the proposed contribution or perspective, and how the authors expect to participate in bridge activities. Submissions will be evaluated based on relevance, clarity, interdisciplinary value, clinical or technical significance, and potential contribution to bridge discussions and shared artifacts. At least one author of each accepted submission must register for AAAI 2027 and attend the bridge in person..

Papers should be formatted according to the AAAI-27 two-column format (author kit). Submissions are handled on OpenReview. We welcome several submission types:.

  • Research Papers – Full Papers (up to 8 pages) and Short Papers (up to 4 pages), describing novel ideas, perspectives, or early research results.
  • Extended Abstracts – up to 2 pages, summarizing late-breaking results, preliminary findings, or challenges to provoke discussion.
  • Demonstrations/Datasets/Tools – up to 4 pages, showcasing innovative systems or prototypes (include a description and optionally a screenshot or link).

All page limits include the references. Selection will emphasize disciplinary breadth and contributions to shared bridge outcomes. Selected contributions will seed short talks, posters, and collaborative working sessions.

Participation Information

Who is this bridge for?

Researchers, practitioners, graduate students, and advanced undergraduates working in agentic AI, planning, knowledge graphs, explainable and causal AI, HCI, software assurance, and healthcare. Tutorials assume general AI literacy; you do not need expertise in every participating field.

What should I submit?

Research Papers – Full papers (up to 8 pages) and Short Papers (up to 4 pages), describing novel ideas, perspectives, or early research results. Extended Abstracts – up to 2 pages, summarizing late-breaking results, preliminary findings, or challenges to provoke discussion. Demonstrations/Datasets/Tools – up to 4 pages, showcasing innovative systems or prototypes (include a description and optionally a screenshot or link). All page limits include the references.

Will the bridge be in person?

Yes. The bridge is planned as a one-day, in-person event in Montréal during the AAAI-27 Bridge Program on February 16 or 17, 2027. The exact day and room will be announced. AAAI requires bridge presenters to attend in person.

Where can I find registration and materials?

Conference registration information will be available through the official AAAI-27 website. Bridge-specific participation instructions and links to tutorial slides, lab materials, and community resources will be posted here as they become available.
